# From CNNs to Transformers: Solving the Distance Problem 

## 1. The Goal 

The main goal was simple:

> **Reduce sequential computation so models can fully use GPUs**

- RNNs  → Sequential (slow, not parallel)
- Need models that can run **many computations at once**

---

## 2. Models Before Transformers 

Some architectures tried to fix this using **CNNs instead of RNNs**:

- **Extended Neural GPU**
- **ByteNet**
- **ConvS2S**

 Important:
These are **model architectures**, not GPUs  
They **use CNNs as their building blocks**

---

## 3. What is a CNN? 

### 🔷 CNN (Convolutional Neural Network)

Originally designed for **images**, but adapted for text.

How it works:

- Uses a **sliding window (kernel)**
- Looks at **small chunks at a time**

### Text Analogy:

Instead of reading one word at a time:

- Look at **3–4 words together**
- Slide across the sentence

---

## 4. CNN vs RNN 


| Feature                  | RNN                          | CNN                                   |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Processing Style         | One step at a time           | Many patches simultaneously            |
| Parallelizable           |  No                         |  Yes                                 |
| Long-range Connections   | Travels through hidden states| Needs many layers to reach far         |
| Designed For             | Sequences                    | Images (adapted for text)              |


## 5. The Distance Problem 

Even though CNNs allow parallel computation, they have a limitation:

 **Connecting far-apart words is hard**

---

### 🔷 ConvS2S (Linear Growth)

- Window sees ~3 words at a time  
- To connect word 1 and word 50:

> Needs ~50 layers  

 Growth = **O(n)** (linear)

---

### 🔷 ByteNet (Logarithmic Growth)

- Uses smarter spacing (dilated convolutions)

> Needs ~log(50) ≈ 6 layers  

Growth = **O(log n)** (better, but still grows)

---

### 🔷 Transformer (Constant Distance)

- Word 1 can directly attend to Word 50 in **ONE step**

 Growth = **O(1)** (constant)

---

## 6. Why This Is Powerful 

- No matter how long the sentence is  
- Every word can directly interact with every other word  

 This removes the **distance bottleneck completely**

---

## 7. The Hidden Cost ⚠️

The Transformer introduces a new problem:

> **Reduced Effective Resolution**

---

### 🧠 Intuition

When attention looks at all words:

- It takes a **weighted average** of all information  
- Everything gets **blended together**

---

### 📸 Analogy

- Clear photo = sharp, specific information  
- Blending 50 photos = blurry image  

👉 Important details can get **lost in the mix**

---

## 8. The Solution: Multi-Head Attention 

Instead of one attention:

👉 Use **multiple attention heads**

Each head focuses on something different:

- Head 1 → Grammar  
- Head 2 → Meaning  
- Head 3 → Position  

---

### 🎥 Analogy

- One camera → blurry group photo  
- Multiple cameras → each captures a clear angle  

👉 Combine them → **sharp, rich understanding**

---

## 9. Final Story Arc 

✅ RNNs → Sequential, slow, not parallelizable  
✅ CNNs → Parallel, but struggle with long-distance connections  
✅ Transformers → Direct connections (O(1), fully parallel)  

⚠️ Problem → Attention blending causes blur  
✅ Solution → Multi-Head Attention restores clarity  

---

## 10. One-Line Insight 

> Transformers solved the distance problem, and multi-head attention solved the clarity problem.

# Table 1: Why Self-Attention?

This section compares four different sequence-processing architectures used in deep learning and explains why the Transformer adopts **self-attention** instead of recurrent or convolutional networks.

The comparison is based on three important metrics:

- **Computational Complexity per Layer**
- **Sequential Operations**
- **Maximum Path Length**

---

# Understanding the Metrics

## 1. Computational Complexity per Layer

This measures how much computation is required to process one layer.

The notation used is:

- **n** = sequence length (number of tokens)
- **d** = model dimension (e.g., 512)
- **k** = convolution kernel size
- **r** = attention window size (restricted attention)

Lower complexity generally means faster execution and lower computational cost.

---

## 2. Sequential Operations

This measures how much of the computation must happen one step after another.

- **O(1)** means the entire sequence can be processed simultaneously.
- **O(n)** means tokens must be processed one after another.

Since GPUs are designed for parallel computation, architectures with fewer sequential operations train much faster.

---

## 3. Maximum Path Length

This measures how many computational steps information must travel between two distant tokens.

For example, consider:

> "The animal didn't cross the street because it was too tired."

To understand that **"it"** refers to **"animal"**, information must travel between those words.

A shorter path allows the model to capture long-range dependencies more easily.

- **O(1)** means any two words communicate directly.
- Larger path lengths require information to pass through multiple intermediate computations.

---

# Why Self-Attention?

## Why This Section Matters

One of the key contributions of the Transformer paper is its justification for replacing recurrent and convolutional architectures with **self-attention**.

Rather than simply claiming that self-attention performs better, the authors compare it against **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)** and **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)** using three objective criteria:

1. Computational complexity per layer
2. Amount of sequential computation
3. Maximum path length between two positions

This comparison explains why the Transformer architecture is significantly faster to train while also being better at modeling long-range dependencies.

---

# The Problem Setup

The comparison considers a sequence-to-sequence transformation where an input sequence

\[
(x_1, x_2, ..., x_n)
\]

is transformed into another sequence

\[
(z_1, z_2, ..., z_n)
\]

of the same length.

This is exactly what happens inside every encoder and decoder layer.

- Each input token \(x_i\) is represented as a vector.
- Each output token \(z_i\) is another transformed vector.
- Every vector has dimension \(d\) (512 in the original Transformer).

The goal is to determine which layer type performs this transformation most efficiently.

---

# The Three Evaluation Criteria

The paper evaluates each architecture using three independent metrics.

These are **evaluation criteria**, not different model architectures.

---

# 1. Computational Complexity per Layer

This metric measures how much computation each layer performs.

It represents the total number of mathematical operations required for one forward pass through a layer.

| Layer | Complexity |
|--------|------------|
| Self-Attention | \(O(n^2 \cdot d)\) |
| Recurrent | \(O(n \cdot d^2)\) |
| Convolutional | \(O(k \cdot n \cdot d^2)\) |

where:

- \(n\) = sequence length
- \(d\) = model dimension
- \(k\) = convolution kernel size

---

## Why Self-Attention Is Faster in Practice

The authors point out that self-attention becomes computationally cheaper than recurrent layers whenever

\[
n < d
\]

For most natural language processing tasks, this condition is almost always satisfied.

Typical values are:

| Quantity | Typical Value |
|----------|---------------|
| Sentence length (\(n\)) | 10–50 words |
| Model dimension (\(d\)) | 512 |

Since

\[
10\text{–}50 < 512
\]

self-attention generally requires less computation than recurrent networks.

---

# 2. Sequential Operations

The second comparison measures **how much of the computation must happen sequentially**.

Some architectures require operations to be performed one after another, while others allow all positions to be processed simultaneously.

| Layer | Sequential Operations |
|--------|----------------------|
| Self-Attention | \(O(1)\) |
| Recurrent | \(O(n)\) |
| Convolutional | \(O(1)\) |
| Restricted Self-Attention | \(O(1)\) |

---

## Why Sequential Computation Matters

Modern GPUs are designed to execute thousands of operations in parallel.

Self-attention fully exploits this capability because every token can be processed at the same time.

For example,

```
Sentence:

I am learning Transformers
```

All words can compute attention simultaneously.

In contrast, an RNN processes tokens sequentially:

```
Word 1
   ↓
Word 2
   ↓
Word 3
   ↓
Word 4
```

Word 4 cannot be processed until Words 1–3 have already been completed.

This sequential dependency limits hardware utilization and significantly increases training time.

---

# 3. Maximum Path Length

The third metric measures the maximum number of computational steps information must travel between two positions in a sequence.

| Layer | Maximum Path Length |
|--------|--------------------|
| Self-Attention | \(O(1)\) |
| Recurrent | \(O(n)\) |
| Convolutional | \(O(\log_k n)\) |
| Restricted Self-Attention | \(O(n/r)\) |

where:

- \(r\) = restricted attention window size

---

# Why Path Length Matters

Long-range dependencies are common in natural language.

Consider the sentence:

> "The trophy didn't fit in the bag because it was too big."

To correctly interpret the sentence, the model must determine whether **"it"** refers to **"trophy"** or **"bag."**

The relevant words are several positions apart.

---

# 4.1 CNN vs Self-Attention — A Deeper Comparison

> **Transformer From Scratch — Tutorial Series**  
> **Section:** `01_story/why_sequence_models_fail.md`

---

# What This Section Is About

After comparing **Self-Attention**, **RNNs**, and **CNNs** using computational complexity, sequential operations, and path length, the paper further analyzes **why convolutional networks are still not the ideal architecture for sequence modeling.**

The goal is to answer an important question:

> **If CNNs are parallelizable like self-attention, why weren't they chosen instead?**

The paper examines several variants of convolution—including standard, dilated, and separable convolutions—and concludes that **none outperform self-attention combined with the position-wise feed-forward network (FFN).**

---

# The Fundamental Limitation of Convolution

A convolution layer processes only a **local neighborhood** around each position.

Suppose we have the sentence:

```
"The cat sat on the mat"
```

Sequence length:

```
n = 6
```

Using a convolution with kernel size:

```
k = 3
```

each convolution only observes three neighboring words at a time.

```
Window 1:
[The, cat, sat]

Window 2:
     [cat, sat, on]

Window 3:
          [sat, on, the]

Window 4:
               [on, the, mat]
```

Notice that:

- **"The"** (position 1)
- **"mat"** (position 6)

are never processed together in a single convolution.

A single CNN layer therefore **cannot directly connect distant positions** when:

```
k < n
```

---

# Why Multiple CNN Layers Are Required

To allow information to travel across the entire sequence, convolutional layers must be stacked.

## Standard Convolution

Each additional layer increases the receptive field only gradually.

The paper shows that connecting all positions requires approximately

```
O(n / k)
```

layers.

Example:

```
Sequence length = 100
Kernel size     = 3

Layers required ≈ 100 / 3 ≈ 33
```

Information must repeatedly pass through intermediate layers before distant words can influence each other.

---

## Dilated Convolution

Dilated convolutions increase the receptive field by skipping positions.

Instead of

```
[1 2 3]
```

the kernel might observe

```
[1   3   5]
```

allowing each layer to cover a larger portion of the sequence.

Because the receptive field grows exponentially, the number of required layers becomes

```
O(logₖ(n))
```

Example:

```
Sequence length = 100
Kernel size     = 3

Layers required ≈ 5
```

This is a significant improvement over standard convolutions.

However, multiple layers are **still necessary** before distant positions can communicate.

---

# Visualizing Standard vs Dilated Convolution

## Standard Convolution

```
Position:
1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8

Kernel:
[■ ■ ■]
   [■ ■ ■]
      [■ ■ ■]
         [■ ■ ■]
```

Only neighboring positions interact.

---

## Dilated Convolution

```
Position:
1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8

Kernel:
[■    ■    ■]
   [■    ■    ■]
```

Each convolution covers a wider region, reducing the number of layers required.

Nevertheless, distant positions are still connected only after several stacked layers.

---

# Self-Attention Solves This Immediately

Self-attention behaves very differently.

Every token attends to **every other token** within the same layer.

```
"The cat sat on the mat"

"The"
   ↕
 "mat"

direct connection
```

Any two words can exchange information in a single attention operation.

Therefore,

```
Maximum Path Length = O(1)
```

No stacking is required.

---

# Computational Cost of CNNs

The computational complexity of a standard convolution layer is

```
O(k · n · d²)
```

where

- **n** = sequence length
- **d** = model dimension
- **k** = kernel width

For recurrent networks:

```
O(n · d²)
```

Since

```
k > 1
```

CNNs require approximately **k times more computation per layer** than recurrent layers.

Examples:

```
k = 3

CNN ≈ 3 × RNN cost
```

```
k = 5

CNN ≈ 5 × RNN cost
```

Standard convolution is therefore more computationally expensive while still requiring multiple stacked layers to model long-range dependencies.

---

# Separable Convolutions

The paper also discusses **separable convolutions**, a more efficient form of convolution.

Instead of performing one large convolution, the computation is factorized into two stages:

1. Spatial processing
2. Channel mixing

The resulting complexity becomes

```
O(k · n · d + n · d²)
```

This is substantially cheaper than

```
O(k · n · d²)
```

used by standard convolutions.

---

# The Best Possible CNN Case

Suppose we maximize the receptive field by setting

```
k = n
```

The complexity becomes

```
O(n² · d + n · d²)
```

Now compare this with the Transformer.

## Self-Attention

```
O(n² · d)
```

## Position-wise Feed-Forward Network

```
O(n · d²)
```

Together:

```
Self-Attention + FFN

O(n² · d + n · d²)
```

This is **identical** to the complexity of the best-case separable convolution.

---

# Comparison

| Property | Separable CNN (k = n) | Self-Attention + FFN |
|-----------|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Complexity | O(n²d + nd²) | O(n²d + nd²) |
| Sequential Operations | O(1) | O(1) |
| Maximum Path Length | O(logₖ(n)) | **O(1)** |
| Interpretability | Low | High |

Even when convolution reaches its most favorable computational complexity, self-attention still provides a shorter communication path between tokens.

---

# Why Path Length Still Matters

Consider the sentence:

```
"The trophy didn't fit in the bag because it was too big."
```

To understand what **"it"** refers to, the model must connect

```
"it"
```

with

```
"trophy"
```

### CNN

Information moves layer by layer through neighboring regions until the two positions become connected.

### Self-Attention

```
"it"
    ↕
"trophy"
```

The dependency is modeled directly within one layer.

Shorter paths allow information and gradients to propagate more effectively during training.

---

# Interpretability

The paper highlights another advantage of self-attention:

> Self-attention produces more interpretable models.

Attention weights explicitly show which words influence each prediction.

Example:

```
Attention("it")

cat     → 0.65
tired   → 0.20
mat     → 0.10
sat     → 0.05
```

This provides a transparent view of the model's reasoning.

By contrast:

- RNN hidden states are dense vectors that are difficult to interpret.
- CNN feature maps reveal local activation patterns but not explicit word-to-word relationships.

Attention visualizations therefore provide valuable insight into what the model has learned.

---

# Overall Comparison

## Compared with RNNs

Self-attention offers:

- Lower computational cost when **n < d**
- Complete parallelization
- Constant path length
- Better handling of long-range dependencies

---

## Compared with Standard CNNs

Self-attention provides:

- Direct communication between all positions
- No need for stacked layers
- Shorter information paths
- No kernel-size multiplier in computational complexity

---

## Compared with Dilated CNNs

Self-attention still offers:

- Constant path length
- Direct global connectivity within a single layer

---

## Compared with Separable CNNs

Even when separable convolutions achieve the same computational complexity,

```
O(n² · d + n · d²)
```

self-attention still has:

- Shorter dependency paths
- Better interpretability
- Direct interaction between all positions

---

# Final Conclusion

The paper concludes that self-attention combined with the position-wise feed-forward network provides the strongest overall architecture for sequence modeling.

While convolutional networks can reduce computation through techniques such as dilation and separable convolutions, they still require multiple layers to establish long-range dependencies. Self-attention connects every position directly within a single layer, enabling efficient learning, complete parallelization, and improved interpretability.
