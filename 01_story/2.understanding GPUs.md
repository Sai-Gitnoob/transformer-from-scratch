# Chapter 2: Understanding GPUs

*Attention solved the "waiting in line" problem. But an idea is only as fast as the hardware running it — so before we go further, let's understand the machine that made the Transformer possible: the GPU.*

---

## 1. Life Before Transformers: Attention + RNN

Attention mechanisms actually existed *before* the Transformer — they just weren't running the show on their own.

👉 They were bolted onto RNNs as a helper:

- The **RNN** still handled the sequence, word by word
- **Attention** helped it focus on the most relevant earlier words while doing so

So attention was the good idea. It just hadn't been set free yet.

---

## 2. The Big Idea

Then came the bold proposal:

> "What if we removed RNNs completely — and used *only* attention?"

That single change meant:

- No more sequential, one-step-at-a-time processing
- No more hidden-state chain where step 50 has to wait for step 49
- **Full parallel computation** — every word processed at once

This became the **Transformer architecture**.

---

## 3. Why Removing the RNN Matters

**The RNN's problem:** if word 1 needs information from word 50, that information has to pass through all 49 steps in between — like a game of telephone, getting a little distorted at each hop.

**Attention's solution:** every word can look directly at every other word, in a single step.

- Word 1 ↔ Word 50 = one direct connection, not 49 hops
- Distance between words stops being a problem

The paper describes this as attention allowing the model to draw dependencies **"without regard to their distance"** in the sequence.

---

## 4. Why This Changes Everything

Because there's no chain of steps waiting on each other, **every word in a sentence can be processed at the same time.** Nothing is standing in line.

That property — full parallelizability — is exactly what modern hardware is built for.

---

## 5. What Is a GPU?

A **GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)** is a processor built specifically to perform *many* calculations simultaneously, rather than one after another.

**A simple analogy:**

- **CPU** — one highly skilled worker, handling tasks one at a time, very capably
- **GPU** — thousands of smaller workers, all working at once

Neural networks lean heavily on huge matrix multiplications, repeated over and over. That's exactly the kind of workload GPUs are built to chew through — far faster than a CPU ever could.

This is why the Transformer's "fully parallel" design wasn't just elegant on paper — it was a perfect match for the hardware already sitting in data centers.

---

## 6. Hardware Then vs. Now

The original Transformer was trained on **8 × NVIDIA P100 GPUs** — top-tier hardware in 2017. Here's how that compares to a modern consumer GPU:

| Spec | P100 (2017) | RTX 5090 (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Memory | 16 GB | 32 GB |
| Memory Bandwidth | ~732 GB/s | ~1,792 GB/s |
| FP16 Tensor Performance | ~21 TFLOPS | ~210 TFLOPS |
| Architecture | Pascal | Blackwell |

*(RTX 5090 figures are per-GPU, dense FP16 Tensor throughput. Real-world training speed depends heavily on the framework and workload, not raw TFLOPS alone.)*

---

## 7. Real-World Impact

- **Original training time:** ~12 hours on 8 × P100 GPUs (base model)
- **On a single modern GPU like the RTX 5090:** each card offers roughly **10× the FP16 throughput** and **~2.4× the memory bandwidth** of a single P100

Even accounting for the fact that the original setup used 8 GPUs working together, a single modern consumer GPU could plausibly train the base model in a small fraction of the original time — this is a rough, illustrative comparison rather than a benchmarked result, but it captures just how much hardware has caught up to the Transformer's appetite for parallel compute.

---

## 8. Why Transformers Scale So Well

**Transformers:**
- Fully parallel by design
- Use GPU power efficiently
- Get *faster* automatically as hardware improves

**RNNs:**
- Sequential by design — one step at a time
- Can't fully exploit a GPU's parallel cores
- Limited speed improvement even on better hardware, because the bottleneck is the *architecture*, not the chip

---

## 9. Final Insight

> Transformers didn't just improve translation accuracy — they were architected for the future of hardware.

- **RNNs** are limited by their own structure, no matter how good the GPU gets
- **Transformers** were built to *use* parallel computing, so they keep getting faster as hardware does

---

## 10. One-Line Summary

> Attention removed the sequential bottleneck, and GPUs unlocked its full power.
